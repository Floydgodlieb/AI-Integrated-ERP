01 · The business workflow

From a sentence to a draft, in seven steps

The orange steps are where a person decides. Later steps, such as confirming the order or posting the invoice, happen in Odoo through the company's normal, authorised process.

1 · YOUdescribe a sale: "Create a sales order for Pepsi with 3 Chair"
2 · AIpicks out the intent, customer, products and quantities
3 · CHECKSthe backend checks the request and finds the names in Odoo
4 · YOUanswer a question if something is missing or unclear
5 · REVIEWOdoo prices, the total and possible duplicates are shown
6 · YOUapprove or reject
7 · ODOOa draft is created and the outcome is recorded

With the seed price of 50.00, the review shows three chairs and 150.00 before tax. Rejecting leaves Odoo unchanged; approving creates a draft quotation and shows its number. Try it below: a replay of the real app's messages, no AI involved.

    03 · The safety fence

    What the AI can and cannot reach

    Whatever the AI says, the backend only lets through what is inside this fence. The Odoo account the assistant uses could technically confirm orders and post invoices; the fence is what stops it.

    04 · A request's journey

    Every way a request can end

    Pick a situation to see the path it takes. Approving creates a draft; it never confirms an order or posts an invoice. Choosing from a list only settles a name; approving to create comes after.

    A network hiccup while creating needs care. If the connection drops during "Creating draft", we cannot be sure whether Odoo saved it. Check Odoo before offering a retry, or the retry could create a second draft. The duplicate warning helps a person spot this, but it is not a guarantee.

    For developers: states in the code

    Approval wait: threading.Event in chat_stream, APPROVAL_TIMEOUT_SECONDS (default 300). A closed browser is recorded as approval_expired with a reason, the same event as a timeout. Declined → result status cancelled. Proposed shared service: accept each approval once, check the owning operator and expiry, and keep the outcome stable on retries (idempotency).

    05 · Data and traceability

    Can we trace a draft back to the sentence that made it?

    Not yet. Two of the three links exist today; the last one is missing. The first fix is small.

    For developers: events and the proposed data model

    Today ai_tool_call, approval_requested and approval_answered carry conv_id; created (model, Odoo ID, values) does not. Conversations and pending approvals are in memory and are lost on restart.

    06 · MCP proposal

    Letting other AI assistants use the same workflow

    MCP is a standard way for an AI assistant to use outside tools. The charter asks that an external assistant can prepare sales through our system. It must go through the same checks as the chat, and a person must still approve.

    For developers: proposed tools and contract

    Tools (names and transport are proposals): lookup_records, prepare_sales_order, prepare_customer_invoice, get_request_status, cancel_request. Preparation returns either clarification information or a pending action ID with review details; get_request_status returns the draft ID after the human decision. Cancelling is safe and may stay a tool.

    The backend receives business intent only: action, customer name, product names, positive quantities. IDs, prices, taxes and record state come from the backend and Odoo; unexpected fields are rejected. Reuse one service for validation, resolution, approval and storage for both MCP and the chat API. The older data-contract draft differs on price overrides; the recorded default is Odoo prices with AI prices off.

      08 · What comes next

      Delivery sequence

      The phases, owners and test gates are in the build plan.

      1. Review with the supervisor: scope, business rules, the data format and who owns MCP.

      2. Freeze the test data and label the sentences: an accuracy set of at least 50 complete sales descriptions, plus a separate set of edge cases.

      3. Traceability and approval checks: the missing link, one approval per request, safe retries.

      4. Build the MCP connection on the shared service and test it with an external assistant.

      5. Measure: matching precision and recall, confidence levels, whole-order accuracy and entry time.

      6. Final check: repeat approve, reject, unclear names, prices and two sessions on the final version; save the evidence and rehearse the demo.

      The 16 November browser test showed five of these checks working with the scripted AI and real Odoo on f6bb128. The real model's accuracy, the final-version results and MCP acceptance are still separate measurements.
